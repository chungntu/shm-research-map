Research Directions

Programme synthesis · 8 November 2026 · 34 projects across 12 research tracks

The programme asks how structural health monitoring can recover unmeasured responses, detect and localize damage, and keep operating when sensors fail or operating conditions shift. The directions below group the projects by the engineering questions they address.

1. Virtual sensing with limited measurements · VS1–VS4, VS8–VS14
VS1 and VS2 estimate quasi-static stress and deck strain on KW51. VS3 asks which lost signals remain recoverable, VS4 studies when sensors should sample under energy constraints, and VS8 relates sensor budget to spectral rank. On Hardanger, VS9 uses dynamic structural condensation and VS11 studies block-Hankel completion and recovery certificates for fixed outage patterns. VS10 develops modal graph sampling in space and time. VS12 tests conformal coverage across retrofit and operational shifts on KW51. VS13 investigates whether a few sensors can recover bending moments along an HDPE fish-cage collar, while VS14 establishes a multi-fidelity surrogate optimization framework (active-learning Gaussian Processes and NSGA-II) to assess storm load-carrying capacity and optimize open-ocean dual-ring HDPE fish cages under typhoon sea states.
2. Damage detection, localization and sensor faults · OC1–OC4, TL1–TL5, FT1, FT3, CW1
OC1–OC3 develop one-class representations on Z24 and AQUINAS. OC4 examines the trade-off between temperature robustness and damage sensitivity on the Sonkyo blade, while CW1 conditions a WaveNet detector on temperature. TL1–TL3 use HBTA topology for edge transfer, sensor restoration and connection localization. TL4 uses transverse modes on Route 345, and TL5 studies physics-informed detection and ensemble localization on LUMO Tower. FT1 uses the wind–traffic crossover to condition one-class reconstruction on Hardanger, while FT3 studies persistent sensor quarantine and detector reconfiguration on HBTA.
3. Transfer across structures and datasets · HP1, PS1
HP1 compares the contribution of input formulation and hyperparameter optimization in supervised damage classification. PS1 studies when knowledge transfer helps across monitored structures and when differences in topology or measurement distributions lead to negative transfer.
4. Structural dynamics, stability and field identification · WT1, EL1–EL2, ST1–ST2
WT1 tests consistency across measurement setups in offshore wind foundation modal identification. EL1 studies two-sided bounds for equivalent linearization, and EL2 examines dissipation and dephasing in measured damping. ST1 investigates when the end portal governs out-of-plane stability on KW51 and Nam Ô. ST2 studies how hanger tension changes lateral restraint and what that means for updating the KW51 model.
5. Vision-based inspection and measurement · CV1–CV3
CV1 uses vision foundation models to detect and segment bridge surface damage. CV2 develops a semi-automatic workflow for labeling multiple damage classes. CV3 uses camera-based, non-contact vibration measurement to extend vision from surface inspection to structural response.
6. Pavement engineering, climate resilience & green transportation · PV1
PV1 establishes the first comprehensive 34-year (1991–2024) nationwide analysis of Weighted Mean Annual Pavement Temperature (WMAPT) across 127 monitoring stations in Vietnam using LTPP InfoPave reanalysis data. It identifies a +0.83°C warming trend, quantifies the spatial divergence in pavement design temperatures between coastal and inland zones (Khánh Hòa case study), and proposes climate-resilient asphalt pavement design guidelines to mitigate rutting and fatigue failures under rising temperatures.

Connections between studies

  • KW51: VS1 and VS2 estimate structural responses. VS12 asks how well prediction intervals hold after retrofit and under changing operating conditions.
  • Hardanger: VS3, VS9 and VS11 examine recoverability and reconstruction under sensor loss. FT1 addresses the downstream task of damage detection.
  • HBTA: TL2 ranks sensors for restoration, FT3 studies how the detector should operate during persistent faults, and TL3 uses the remaining modal information to localize connection damage.
